# Alpha Detection Applications

What you can detect with an alpha-capable tube (like LND-712) that you **cannot** detect with beta/gamma-only tubes (like SBM-20).

Alpha particles are stopped by:
- A sheet of paper
- A few cm of air
- Dead skin cells
- The glass window of an SBM-20

This makes alpha-capable detectors both **more sensitive** to certain sources and **more specific** for certain types of contamination.

---

## 1. Radon and Radon Progeny Detection

**Most practical application for home safety**

### What is Radon?

- Radon-222 is a radioactive noble gas produced by uranium-238 decay
- Seeps from soil/rock into basements and crawl spaces
- **Second leading cause of lung cancer** after smoking (EPA)
- Colorless, odorless, tasteless — impossible to detect without instruments
- Present in **every** home to some degree

### Why Alpha Detection Matters

Radon itself is a gas and hard to measure directly, but its **decay products** (radon progeny) stick to dust particles and surfaces:

- **Polonium-218** (α emitter, 3.1 minutes half-life)
- **Polonium-214** (α emitter, 164 µs half-life)
- **Polonium-210** (α emitter, 138 days half-life)

These emit **strong alpha radiation** but very little gamma. A beta/gamma tube will barely detect them.

### Experiments You Can Do

**Test #1: Basement Air Quality**
1. Place LND-712 detector in basement for 24+ hours
2. Compare CPM to outdoor reading
3. Ratio >3:1 suggests elevated radon levels
4. Follow up with proper radon test kit if high

**Test #2: Filter Collection**
1. Run HVAC or vacuum cleaner for 30 minutes
2. Remove filter
3. Place LND-712 directly on filter surface
4. Radon daughters accumulate on dust particles
5. You'll see 5-10× higher counts on filter vs. air

**Test #3: Static Electricity Collection**
1. Rub a balloon on your hair to charge it
2. Leave balloon in basement for 1 hour (collects charged radon daughters)
3. Hold LND-712 near balloon
4. Strong alpha counts indicate radon presence

**Test #4: Wipe Test**
1. Wipe basement window sill with coffee filter
2. Place LND-712 directly on filter
3. Alpha counts show radon daughter deposition

### What SBM-20 Shows
- **Background levels only** — misses 80-90% of the radon signal
- Cannot distinguish radon from other sources
- Useless for radon monitoring

### Why It Matters
- EPA action level: 4 pCi/L (148 Bq/m³)
- Radon causes ~21,000 lung cancer deaths/year in US
- Mitigation (fan + pipe) is simple and effective
- **Your LND-712 can screen for problems before paying for a lab test**

## 2. Thorium Detection (Extremely Common in Vintage Items)

**Thorium-232 is surprisingly common in everyday objects**

### Sources Rich in Thorium

**1. Camping Lantern Mantles**
- Gas lantern mantles (Coleman, etc.) are **thorium-impregnated fabric**
- Used to produce bright white light when heated
- ~300-400 mg thorium per mantle
- **Extremely hot** alpha source (10,000-50,000 CPM on LND-712)
- SBM-20 barely detects them (~100-200 CPM, mostly beta/gamma)

**Experiment:**
- Place unburned mantle next to LND-712: 20,000+ CPM
- Place sheet of paper between mantle and tube: drops to ~200 CPM (alphas blocked)
- Burn the mantle: activity drops 50% (thorium oxide becomes less friable)

**2. Vintage Orange Fiestaware (Pre-1972)**
- Uranium glaze (detectable by SBM-20)
- But newer reproductions use **thorium** instead
- LND-712 distinguishes: thorium = strong alpha, uranium = weaker alpha

**3. Welding Electrodes (Tungsten)**
- 2% thoriated tungsten electrodes (still sold today)
- Used in TIG welding
- Strong alpha emitter when ground (produces radioactive dust)
- **Safety concern:** welders inhale thorium dust
- LND-712 can detect contamination on grinder, workbench, clothing

**4. Vintage Camera Lenses**
- 1940s-1970s camera lenses used thorium glass
  - Yellows with age (radiation damage)
  - Examples: Kodak Aero-Ektar, Pentax Super-Takumar 50mm f/1.4
- Strong alpha emission from glass surface
- SBM-20 shows almost nothing

**Experiment:**
- Hold LND-712 touching lens: 500-2000 CPM
- Hold SBM-20 touching lens: 50-100 CPM (barely above background)

**5. Vintage Gas Mantles (Pre-1990)**
- Not just camping — also **decorative gas lamps**
- Antique stores are full of these
- Some contain radium instead of thorium (even hotter)

**6. Monazite Sand**
- Naturally occurring thorium ore
- Found on certain beaches (North Carolina, India, Brazil)
- Black/brown heavy mineral sand
- Can be 10-100× background on LND-712
- Almost invisible to SBM-20

### What You Learn
- **Alpha detection = instant thorium identification**
- SBM-20 cannot distinguish thorium from uranium
- Safety screening for welding shops, antique stores

## 3. Smoke Detector Americium-241

**Every ionization smoke detector contains a radioactive source**

### What's Inside
- Americium-241 (α emitter, 432 year half-life)
- ~1 µCi (37 kBq) activity
- Ionizes air to detect smoke particles
- Contained in a small metal chamber

### Detection
- **LND-712**: 5,000-10,000 CPM when held near the detector opening
- **SBM-20**: 20-50 CPM (barely detectable, mostly from gamma)

### Experiments

**Test #1: Inverse Square Law**
1. Remove smoke detector from ceiling (disable AC power)
2. Measure CPM at 1 cm, 2 cm, 4 cm, 8 cm distances
3. Observe 1/r² falloff (alpha range in air)
4. Plot distance vs. CPM on log-log paper

**Test #2: Alpha Range in Air**
1. Position LND-712 at maximum count rate (~1 cm from source)
2. Slowly move away while recording CPM
3. Alpha counts drop to background at ~5-7 cm
4. Demonstrates why alphas are "safe" externally but dangerous internally

**Test #3: Shielding Effectiveness**
- Paper: blocks 99% of alpha
- Plastic wrap: blocks 90-95%
- Aluminum foil: blocks 100%
- Your hand: blocks 100% (dead skin layer is enough)

**Test #4: Half-Life Verification**
- Extract Am-241 foil (requires disassembly — be careful)
- Measure CPM monthly for a year
- Calculate decay constant
- Verify 432-year half-life (you won't see measurable decay, but demonstrates the concept)

### Safety Note
- Am-241 is **only dangerous if inhaled or ingested**
- Don't disassemble smoke detectors unless you know what you're doing
- Keep source sealed
- Don't sand, drill, or damage the source foil

## 4. Depleted Uranium (DU) Detection

**DU is used in surprising places**

### What is Depleted Uranium?
- Uranium-238 with most U-235 removed
- 40% less radioactive than natural uranium
- **But still a strong alpha emitter**
- Used for density, not radioactivity

### Where You Find It

**1. Aircraft Counterweights**
- Boeing 747 (pre-1981): ~1,500 kg of DU
- Many vintage aircraft
- Often sold as scrap without proper labeling
- **Alpha hot, gamma cold** (characteristic signature)

**2. Military Surplus**
- Tank armor penetrators (highly regulated)
- Armor plating (rare civilian access)
- Radiation shielding (medical/industrial)

**3. Industrial Shielding**
- X-ray and gamma-ray shielding containers
- Some vintage radiation therapy equipment
- Oil well logging tools

**4. Sailing Yacht Keels (Rare)**
- A few luxury yachts used DU for ballast (1970s-1980s)
- Extremely heavy, low center of gravity
- Controversial and mostly removed

### Why LND-712 Matters
- DU emits mostly **alpha** and weak gamma
- **SBM-20**: 50-100 CPM on contact (barely detectable)
- **LND-712**: 5,000-10,000 CPM on contact (unmistakable)
- Allows positive identification before handling

### Practical Use Case
- Aviation surplus yards
- Military surplus stores
- Scrapyards
- "Is this mystery metal uranium?" → LND-712 gives instant answer

## 5. Distinguishing Contamination Types

**Alpha detection tells you WHERE the contamination is**

### Key Principle
- Alphas travel <5 cm in air
- Cannot penetrate skin
- **Surface contamination only**

### What This Means

**Scenario 1: Lab Contamination**
- Gamma detector shows elevated readings in a room
- Could be:
  - **Surface contamination** (cleanup needed)
  - **Internal contamination** (inside walls, pipes)
  - **External source** (neighboring room, stored sources)

**With LND-712:**
- Scan surfaces with tube close to material
- Alpha counts = **surface contamination** (wipe it up)
- No alpha counts = contamination is inside/behind surfaces or external

**Scenario 2: Used Equipment**
- Buying surplus lab equipment
- Seller says "decontaminated"
- Gamma detector shows low background
- **But LND-712 shows high alpha counts on surfaces** → not actually clean

### Wipe Testing
1. Wipe suspected surface with damp paper towel
2. Hold LND-712 directly on towel
3. Alpha counts = removable contamination
4. Gamma-only tube would miss this entirely

### Personnel Contamination
- After working with alpha sources (uranium minerals, radium dials, etc.)
- Check hands, clothing, work surface with LND-712
- Alpha counts = wash it off NOW
- Critical for safety (ingestion/inhalation risk)

## 6. Radium Watch Dials and Instruments

**Vintage glow-in-the-dark items used radium-226**

### History
- 1900s-1960s: radium paint for luminous dials
  - Watches (especially military)
  - Aircraft instruments
  - Compasses
  - Clocks
  - Gun sights
- Radium-226 (α, β, γ emitter, 1,600 year half-life)
- Mixed with phosphor to glow continuously

### Why Alpha Detection Matters

**Intact Dials:**
- Glass/crystal blocks alphas
- **SBM-20**: 100-500 CPM near dial (mostly gamma from radium daughters)
- **LND-712**: Same 100-500 CPM (alphas blocked by glass)

**Damaged/Open Dials:**
- Radium paint flaking off
- **SBM-20**: 500-1,000 CPM (gamma)
- **LND-712**: **10,000-50,000 CPM** (alphas escaping)
- **Critical safety difference**: shows when radium is becoming airborne

### Contamination Detection
- Radium paint flakes off and spreads
- **Invisible to the eye**
- **Invisible to beta/gamma detector** (small amount, far from tube)
- **Visible to alpha detector** (direct surface contact)

**Experiment:**
1. Wipe surface of vintage watch case with coffee filter
2. Test filter with LND-712
3. Strong alpha counts = radium contamination
4. Seal watch in plastic bag, clean workspace

### Safety Critical
- Radium is **extremely toxic** (bone-seeker)
- Radium factory workers died from ingesting paint (lip-pointing brushes)
- Alpha detector shows when contamination is present
- Gamma-only detector misses it until it's too late

**Real-world case:**
- Antique dealer buys collection of radium watches
- Stores them in wooden drawer
- Radium paint dust contaminates everything
- Gamma detector shows "safe" levels
- **Alpha detector shows heavy contamination**
- Requires professional cleanup

## 7. Natural Uranium Minerals

**Uranium minerals are surprisingly common**

### Collectible Minerals
- **Autunite** (uranium phosphate, bright yellow/green)
- **Torbernite** (uranium copper phosphate, green crystals)
- **Uraninite/Pitchblende** (uranium oxide, black)
- **Carnotite** (uranium vanadium oxide, yellow powder)
- **Zippeite** (uranium sulfate, yellow/orange)

### Why Alpha Detection Matters
- Fresh fracture surfaces emit strong alphas
- Weathered surfaces may have alpha-blocking patina
- **SBM-20**: Shows 200-2,000 CPM depending on size
- **LND-712**: Shows **5,000-50,000 CPM** on contact
  - 10-25× higher due to alpha detection

### Experiments

**Test #1: Surface vs. Bulk Activity**
1. Measure CPM on weathered surface
2. Break mineral to expose fresh interior
3. Measure CPM on fresh surface
4. Fresh surface is 2-5× hotter (alphas escape more easily)

**Test #2: Alpha Self-Absorption**
- Alphas are absorbed by the mineral itself
- Large crystal vs. powder:
  - Powder: 5× more activity per gram (more surface area)
  - Crystal: alphas absorbed internally
- Demonstrates alpha range in solids

**Test #3: Age Dating (Advanced)**
- Uranium-238 → Thorium-234 → ... → Lead-206
- Alpha activity vs. lead content → mineral age
- Requires chemistry and precise measurements
- Possible with LND-712 + alpha spectrometry setup

### Mineral Hunting
- Abandoned uranium mines (Colorado, Utah, Arizona)
- Specific geological formations
- **LND-712 can detect weak mineralization that SBM-20 misses**
- Find uranium veins in rock outcrops

## 8. Polonium-210 Detection (Rare but Important)

**Most famous alpha emitter after radon**

### What is Po-210?
- Pure alpha emitter (no beta, minimal gamma)
- 138-day half-life
- **Most toxic substance on Earth** by weight
- Used in nuclear batteries, industrial static eliminators
- Infamous assassination weapon (Litvinenko case, 2006)

### Sources (All Rare/Regulated)
- **Static eliminators** (antique camera/vinyl record brushes)
- **Thermoelectric generators** (space probes)
- **Neutron initiators** (nuclear weapons)
- **Tobacco smoke** (trace amounts from fertilizer)

### Detection
- **Completely invisible to SBM-20** (pure alpha, minimal gamma)
- **LND-712**: Extremely high CPM on contact
- Only way to detect Po-210 without alpha-sensitive equipment

### Why It Matters
- Po-210 contamination is **silent** to gamma detectors
- Security/forensic applications
- Safety screening of vintage static eliminators

**Real-world example:**
- Vintage Staticmaster brush (contains Po-210)
- SBM-20 shows background levels
- LND-712 shows 20,000+ CPM
- User has no idea they're handling one of the most toxic substances on Earth

## 9. Air Filter Analysis

**Collect airborne radioactivity**

### Why Air Filters
- Radon daughters attach to dust
- Fission products from nuclear tests/accidents attach to particles
- HVAC filters concentrate airborne radioactivity

### Experiments

**Test #1: Home HVAC Filter**
1. Remove 1-month-old furnace filter
2. Cut 10cm × 10cm sample from dirtiest area
3. Place LND-712 directly on filter surface
4. Record CPM
5. Typical: 50-200 CPM above background (radon daughters)
6. If >500 CPM, consider radon testing

**Test #2: Geographic Variation**
- Collect filters from different locations (friends/family)
- Compare alpha activity
- Maps to regional radon levels + geology
- Colorado/Pennsylvania: high
- Florida/Texas: low

**Test #3: Vacuum Cleaner Bag**
- Vacuum bedroom for 30 minutes
- Remove dust bag
- Test with LND-712
- Shows accumulated radon daughters + any contamination tracked in

**Test #4: Car Cabin Filter**
- Cars accumulate radon daughters from ventilation
- Remove cabin air filter (check owner's manual)
- Strong alpha signal = radon in garage or high environmental levels

**Test #5: Post-Nuclear-Incident Monitoring**
- After Fukushima, Chernobyl, or nuclear tests
- Fission products (I-131, Cs-137, Sr-90, Pu-239) deposited globally
- Alpha activity on filters shows actinide contamination (plutonium, americium)
- Historical record: test vintage filters from Cold War era

### What SBM-20 Shows
- Weak signal from radon daughters (mostly gamma)
- Cannot distinguish radon from other sources
- Misses 80% of the signal

## 10. Testing Drinking Water for Alpha Emitters

**Uranium and radium dissolve in groundwater**

### Why It Matters
- Uranium in well water (common in granite areas)
- Radium in deep wells (oil/gas regions)
- EPA standard: 15 pCi/L for alpha emitters in drinking water

### Simple Test (Not Certified, but Useful)

1. **Collect 1 liter of water** from well/tap
2. **Evaporate water** in glass dish (hot plate, 100°C, 4-6 hours)
3. **Residue** contains minerals including any dissolved uranium/radium
4. **Test residue with LND-712** directly on dish surface
5. **Background + 50-100 CPM** = probably elevated uranium
6. **Background + 500+ CPM** = definitely elevated, get lab test

### What You're Detecting
- Dissolved uranium, radium, thorium, polonium
- These emit alphas
- SBM-20 would show almost nothing (dissolved solids are thin layer)

### Practical Use
- **Screen well water** before sending expensive lab sample
- **Monitor changes** over time (geology, plumbing)
- **Compare water sources** (well vs. municipal, before/after filtration)

### Follow-Up
- If elevated: send sample to certified lab
- Reverse osmosis removes 95-99% of uranium/radium
- Test filtered water vs. unfiltered

## 11. Distinguishing Uranium from Thorium

**Both are alpha emitters, but different gamma signatures**

### The Problem
- Uranium and thorium both emit alpha particles
- Both are found in minerals, glass, ceramics
- Gamma-only detector can sometimes distinguish (U-238 has more gamma)
- But weak sources or shielded sources are hard to tell apart

### With LND-712
**Step 1: Confirm Alpha Activity**
- Strong alpha = uranium or thorium (rules out other sources)

**Step 2: Energy Discrimination (Advanced)**
- Alpha spectrometry (requires more than just LND-712)
- Uranium-238: 4.2 MeV alphas
- Thorium-232: 4.0 MeV alphas
- Different energies = different range in air
- Measure activity vs. distance → identify source

**Step 3: Gamma Spectroscopy (Requires NaI or HPGe detector)**
- Use LND-712 to confirm alpha source
- Use gamma spectrometer to identify isotope
- Combined: complete identification

### Practical Examples
- **Fiestaware**: Strong alpha + U-238 gamma peaks = uranium glaze
- **Camera lens**: Strong alpha + Th-232 gamma peaks = thorium glass
- **Lantern mantle**: Extremely strong alpha + weak gamma = thorium oxide

## 12. Prospecting and Geology

**Find uranium deposits**

### Field Use
- Hiking in uranium-bearing regions (Colorado Plateau, Wyoming, etc.)
- Look for yellow/green staining on rocks
- Test with LND-712:
  - **SBM-20**: 100-500 CPM (might miss weak deposits)
  - **LND-712**: 1,000-10,000 CPM (even weak deposits are obvious)

### Why Alpha Detection Helps
- Uranium ore often has thick coating/weathering
- Gamma rays penetrate, but many are absorbed
- Alphas escape from surface → high count rate on direct contact
- **5-10× more sensitive** than gamma-only tube

### Mineral Identification
- Unknown yellow mineral in rock
- High alpha + high gamma = likely uranium
- High alpha + low gamma = likely thorium
- Low alpha + moderate gamma = likely potassium (K-40)

## 13. Archaeological Dating (Advanced)

**Alpha damage in ancient materials**

### Principle
- Uranium/thorium in pottery, glass, minerals emit alphas
- Alphas damage crystal structure over thousands of years
- Damage accumulates linearly with age
- Can be measured with TL/OSL dating

### What LND-712 Does
- Confirms presence of alpha emitters
- Quantifies alpha activity
- Input to age calculation

### Applications
- Dating ancient glass (Roman, Egyptian)
- Dating pottery glazes
- Dating geological samples (minerals, rocks)
- Verifying authenticity (fake antiquities have wrong alpha signature)

## 15. Advanced: Alpha Spectroscopy

**Identify specific isotopes**

### Concept
- Different isotopes emit alphas at different energies
- Uranium-238: 4.2 MeV
- Radium-226: 4.8 MeV
- Polonium-210: 5.3 MeV
- Americium-241: 5.5 MeV

### Setup (Beyond Basic LND-712)
- LND-712 as detector
- Pulse height analysis circuit
- MCA (multichannel analyzer)
- Vacuum chamber (alphas absorbed by air)

### What You Can Do
- **Identify unknown sources**
- **Measure isotope ratios** (U-235/U-238)
- **Detect contamination** (Pu-239 in uranium)
- **Age date materials** (daughter products accumulate)

### DIY Projects
- Build alpha spectrometer with LND-712 + Arduino + vacuum chamber
- ~$200 in parts
- 1-2 MeV resolution (enough to separate major peaks)

## Practical Considerations

### When Alpha Detection is Critical
- **Radon monitoring** (health risk)
- **Contamination control** (safety)
- **Source identification** (thorium vs. uranium)
- **Radium item safety** (handling)

### When Beta/Gamma is Sufficient
- **Environmental monitoring** (external exposure)
- **Shielded sources** (medical, industrial)
- **Distance detection** (alphas don't travel)
- **Through-material scanning** (alphas blocked by anything)

### Best Practice
- **Use both types**:
  - LND-712 for contamination/surface/identification
  - Beta/gamma tube for environmental/distance/shielded sources
- **Complementary information** = complete picture

1. **Radon screening** (health/safety)
2. **Contamination detection** (safety-critical)

For your uranium glass, the LND-712 would show roughly **double the count rate** because it detects both the gamma rays AND the alpha particles that your SBM-20 is currently missing.

The upgrade from SBM-20 to LND-712 isn't just "more sensitive" — it's **fundamentally different information**.
